# Perch 1.2: whole-app review

Status: pending. The user explicitly assigned the full review **and its resulting changes** to version 1.2 on 2026-09-06. The 1.1 local feature release is recorded in [RELEASE-1.1.md](RELEASE-1.1.md). Incremental fixes and safe test passes do not constitute completion of this review.

## Required perspectives

- Design: hierarchy, density, typography, warning prominence, light/dark and increased-contrast appearances.
- Usability and intuitiveness: discoverability, understandable labels, predictable toggles, visible current state, and first-run experience.
- Flow: one-window settings navigation, returning from child pages, permission handoffs, focus, cancellation, password prompts, and shortcut testing.
- Clarity: current state versus intended action, prerequisites versus optional features, honest status and errors, and consistent units.
- Performance: idle/menu-open/input/process-burst costs, wakeups, status publication, collector backlog, and attribution of Perch versus eslogger costs.
- Memory: bounded parsing/queues/history, resource lifetimes, long-running growth, and recovery after bursts.
- Security: least privilege, signing and helper identity, local-message authentication, file ownership, process identity checks, panic exclusions, and privacy-reset sequencing.
- Reliability and recovery: missing permissions, stale helpers, reconnect/wake, app updates, partial failures, and safe fallback behavior.
- Feature set and bloat: whether each feature serves Perch's purpose, duplicated controls, unnecessary setup, and ongoing maintenance cost.
- Accessibility and test coverage: keyboard operation, readable status beyond color alone, safe automated coverage, and explicit limits of hardware testing.

## Deliverable

Record concrete findings with severity, user impact, evidence, and recommended changes. Distinguish measured results from assumptions and hardware checks still needed. Prioritize and fix the agreed findings, then verify the resulting version. Do not label 1.2 complete solely because an automated suite passes.

## Distribution and Homebrew (requested 2026-09-06)

Investigate how other people can install and use Perch, including Homebrew publication. This is an explicit 1.2 task, not authorization to publish anything now.

- Choose an initial distribution route: a Homebrew tap with an app cask, or a source-build formula where appropriate; assess eligibility and maintenance requirements for the main Homebrew repositories later.
- Replace machine-specific identifiers, local certificate assumptions, paths and installer ownership with a distributable setup. Decide signing/notarization and release/update strategy; explain the practical limits of distributing without a paid Apple Developer identity.
- Verify installation, required permissions, collector/helper ownership, login behavior, upgrades, rollback and uninstall on a clean Mac/account. Perch must receive its own grants; never require users to weaken SIP or grant their development tools extra permissions.
- Make eslogger/macOS compatibility and any distribution/entitlement limits explicit. Confirm that the features shipped actually work on supported Macs without relying on this development machine’s grants or launch jobs.
- Audit dependencies and licenses, prepare release artifacts and checksums, document supported systems and recovery, and choose a sustainable keyboard-profile/catalog update process.
- Prepare a concrete release/cask for review before any public upload, tap publication or upstream submission.

The requested monitor-input hotkey is intended as the final feature before this formal review, subject to monitor/cable/DDC support; see `MONITOR-INPUTS.md`.

Starting references for packaging: [Homebrew Cask Cookbook](https://docs.brew.sh/Cask-Cookbook) and [maintaining a tap](https://docs.brew.sh/How-to-Create-and-Maintain-a-Tap).
